## Module 1 — Serbian for Lyrics **[CORE + SEED banks]**

### 1.1 Dialect: ekavica vs ijekavica (pick one per persona, hold it)

| Concept | Ekavica (Serbia) | Ijekavica (Bosnia/MNE) |
|---|---|---|
| nice | lepo | lijepo |
| milk | mleko | mlijeko |
| time | vreme | vrijeme |
| child | dete | dijete |
| white | belo | bijelo |
| river | reka | rijeka |
| song/poem | pesma | pjesma |
| place | mesto | mjesto |
| where | gde | gdje |
| want (I) | hoću / 'oću | hoću / 'oću *(shared)* |

> The `je/ije` reflex of old *yat* is the tell. Some words are shared (`noć`, `voda`). Bosnian also leans on more turcizmi (Module 3.4).

### 1.2 Spoken contractions & elisions (encouraged — they make lines sing)

| Full | Sung / spoken | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| šta | š'a | very common |
| hajde | 'ajde / aj | call to action |
| hoću / hoćeš | 'oću / 'oćeš | drop initial h |
| kao | ka'o / k'o | simile workhorse |
| rekao / došao / otišao | rek'o / doš'o / otiš'o | masc. past -ao → -'o |
| je li | je l' / jel' | question opener |
| nemoj | nemo' | |
| ne mogu | ne mogu / nemrem *(dial.)* | |
| jesi li | jes' / jesi l' | |
| ne znam | nezn'm / ne znam | |

Use these to hit syllable counts and to sound human — **not** randomly. Over-contraction reads as caricature.

### 1.3 Register map (keep one lane per persona)

- **Street / colloquial:** slang, contractions, turcizmi, profanity (genre-permitting). Drill, trap, club.
- **Modern pop neutral:** clean colloquial, light slang, accessible. Mainstream pop.
- **Lyrical / sevdah-tinged:** restrained, image-led, turcizmi for warmth, no slang. Ballads, sevdah-pop.
- **Forbidden in modern voices unless code-switching:** archaic/literary (`ljubljena, milina, obasjava, čežnja, ushit`).

### 1.4 Slang & turcizmi banks **[SEED — expand per region/era]**

- **General slang:** bre, faca, ekipa, riba, dečko/dečkо, lova/keš/pare, mrak *(=cool)*, ludnica, frka, smor, kreja, šljaka, klinac, matori, brate/braco, kume, ortak.
- **Turcizmi (warmth, sevdah, everyday):** merak, sevdah, dert, ćeif, sokak, avlija, čaršija, baksuz, džaba, komšija, jastuk, jorgan, rakija, kafa, sevap, dušman, jok, aman.
- **Belgrade-leaning:** bre, more, deki, splav, Taš, keva/ćale.
- **Bosnian-leaning:** more turcizmi, *bona/bolan*, *jaran*, *fol*, *haman*.
- **Drill/trap imports (use as tags, not narrative):** opp, dril, švaler, drop, kriza, plug.

> **Rule of use:** every slang/turcizam must do work in the line. Decoration = slop (Playbook §E3).

## Module 2 — Prosody & Rhyme **[CORE]**

### 2.1 Stress, for lyricists
Standard (Štokavian) Serbian **never stresses the final syllable of a polysyllabic word.** Monosyllables can take the beat. Practical rule: put the *natural* word stress on the strong beat; if you're bending stress to land a rhyme, the line is wrong (Playbook §C4).

### 2.2 The rhyme-quality ladder (climb above the cheap rungs)

1. **Cheap — grammatical rhyme:** matching case/verb endings (`-ama, -ima, -ao, -ću`). Free in Serbian → sounds amateur. **Cap 25%** (Constitution Art. 3.1).
2. **Better — root/content rhyme:** the rhyming sound sits on the meaningful part of different-root words.
3. **Strong — rich rhyme:** same stressed vowel + following sounds, *different* preceding consonants (`grad / sad` is thin; aim richer).
4. **Texture — slant / assonance / consonance:** near-rhymes that keep momentum without the sing-song of perfect endings. Heavy in good rap and modern pop.
5. **Speed — internal rhyme:** rhymes inside the line, the engine of dense rap flows.

> Vary the rungs within a song. An all-perfect-rhyme song sounds like a nursery; an all-slant song can feel unmoored. Mix with intent.

### 2.3 Singability
- Write to a **stated tempo and phrase length.** Say every line out loud against it.
- **Open vowels (a, o)** carry held notes and melisma — favor them on sustained syllables (turbo-folk's oriental melodic runs need open vowels).
- Keep consonant clusters off the fastest syllables.
- Verse lines can be dense; chorus lines must be **chant-able** (fewer tokens, bigger vowels).

### 2.4 Line-length targets (starting grid; genre modules refine)

| Section | Tokens/line | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Verse | 5–8 | story, image, specifics |
| Pre-chorus | 4–6 | tension, breath build |
| Chorus / hook | 3–5 | chant, singalong, the thesis |
| Bridge | 4–6 | the turn — contrast or twist |

## Module 3 — Imagery & Lexicon Banks **[SEED — expand heavily]**

Concrete material to pull from. **Always prefer a concrete noun to an abstract one** (Constitution Art. 2).

### 3.1 Concrete noun banks by theme
- **Heartbreak / absence:** hladna kafa, prazna strana kreveta, neodgovorena poruka, broj u telefonu, ugašena cigareta, tvoj parfem na jastuku, ključ koji više ne treba.
- **Night / club:** splav, neon, separe, šank, led u čaši, bina, reflektor, taksi u 4 ujutru, znoj, bas, mrak.
- **Money / status:** lanac, zlato, sat, keš, sef, beli auto, avion, rezervisan sto, torba, patike.
- **City / place:** Beograd, Sava, Dunav, Kalemegdan, Ada, Knez Mihailova, Novi Sad, Niš; Sarajevo, Baščaršija, Miljacka, Grbavica.
- **Defiance / inat:** vrata koja lupam, broj koji brišem, čaša koju dižem sama, korak koji ne staje.

### 3.2 Balkan emotional motif glossary (concepts that don't translate cleanly)
- **inat** — defiant spite; doing a thing *because* you were told not to. The Balkan superpower.
- **sevdah** — sweet, aching longing; melancholy you almost enjoy.
- **merak** — deep pleasure/contentment in a small thing (coffee, a song, a view).
- **dert** — heavy private sorrow you carry.
- **baksuz** — bad luck / a jinx.
- **ćeif/ćef** — whim, mood, the pleasure of doing as you please.

> These are *engines*, not vocabulary to name. Build a scene that *is* sevdah; don't write the word "sevdah."

### 3.3 Native idiom bank (use instead of calques)
- ludim za tobom · puklo mi je · pao mi mrak na oči · srce na mestu · ne mogu da te skinem s očiju · gori mi pod nogama · nema me · pukla tikva · boli me uvo · sve mi se smučilo.

### 3.4 Turcizmi for warmth — see Module 1.4.

## Module 4 — Genre Craft **[CORE]**

Each genre: **ethos** (what it's really about), **structure**, **do**, **don't**, **signature moves**. These refine the generic grid in 2.4 and the existing [`reports/style_guide.md`](../reports/style_guide.md) / [`style_guide_rap.md`](../reports/style_guide_rap.md).

### 4.1 Pop-folk / turbo-folk
- **Ethos:** emotional maximalism — heartbreak, jealousy, defiance, luxury, sex appeal, *"jača sam od tebe."*
- **Structure:** verse → pre-chorus build → big chorus; often a key-change lift late; call-and-response.
- **Do:** open vowels for melisma; one strong central image; the diva's swagger; concrete luxury/night detail.
- **Don't:** literary register; understatement where the genre wants a punch.
- **Signature moves:** the dramatic late key change; the chorus that doubles as a toast/declaration.

### 4.2 Tech-house / club pop
- **Ethos:** the body and the floor; hypnosis through the hook; less *story*, more *spell*.
- **Structure:** minimal verse → chant hook → the drop; layered repetition with ad-libs.
- **Do:** short chantable hook; body/dance/night imagery; earned repetition; tasteful code-switch.
- **Don't:** narrative clutter; English carrying meaning; repetition of a forgettable line.
- **Signature moves:** the pre-drop tension line; the one-word/phonetic hook (e.g. *"origami"*).

### 4.3 Hip-hop / trap
- **Ethos:** flow + persona; boast, story, or diss; identity through specifics.
- **Structure:** intro tag → verse 16–24 bars → hook → verse 2 (often feature) → hook → out.
- **Do:** dense internal & multisyllabic rhyme; brand/street/city specifics; ad-libs as texture; contrast verse density vs. hook chant.
- **Don't:** English in narrative lines; generic flexing with no named detail.
- **Signature moves:** the ad-lib echo; the cadence switch mid-verse.

### 4.4 Drill
- **Ethos:** menace, cold confidence, minimalism; the energy is in restraint and the beat switch.
- **Structure:** hook often opens; sparse hard-hitting bars; triplet flows on sliding 808s.
- **Do:** short punchy lines (2–4 tokens on half-time); cold concrete images; controlled edge.
- **Don't:** sanitize into toothlessness (Constitution Art. 7.1) — and don't cross into real-world targeted threats/slurs.
- **Signature moves:** the beat switch; the half-said threat that implies more than it states.

### 4.5 Sevdah / sevdah-pop (the authenticity benchmark)
- **Ethos:** longing carried by a single image; the unspoken does the work; restraint is power.
- **Structure:** spare, repetitive, hypnotic; melody-led.
- **Do:** one telling detail (an empty chair, a fading song); turcizmi for warmth; *show, never announce.*
- **Don't:** explain the feeling; pile abstractions; rush.
- **Signature move:** the withheld line — the emotion that lands *because* it's never named. Borrow this engine into any genre to beat slop.

## Module 5 — Persona Library **[SEED — expand; every song picks one]**

A persona is a **locked voice**: region/dialect, genre, themes, vocabulary fingerprint, and a *"would never say"* list. Writing *as* a persona is the single biggest anti-slop lever (Constitution Art. 5.1).

### Persona spec template
```
ID · Name · One-line identity
Region / Dialect: (city → ekavica | ijekavica)
Genre lane: (pop-folk | tech-house | trap | drill | sevdah-pop)
Age / attitude:
Core themes: (2–3)
Vocabulary fingerprint: (10–15 signature words/phrases they actually use)
Imagery world: (their concrete objects/places)
Rhyme/flow signature:
WOULD NEVER SAY: (the anti-list — words/moves that break character)
Reference calibration: (corpus artists in the neighborhood — for tone, NOT to copy)
```

## Module 6 — Songwriting Methodology **[CORE]**

General craft, applied to Serbian. These are *how the pros work*, not genre trivia.

- **One idea per song.** A song is one emotional truth, not three. Decide it before drafting (the concept sheet, see [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md) workflow).
- **The title is the song.** The central phrase/hook is the thesis; every line earns its place around it.
- **Object writing (sense-bound).** Start from a *specific moment* and write what's seen/heard/touched/smelled/tasted. This is the antidote to abstraction (Playbook §B1).
- **Show, don't tell.** Carry emotion through a concrete scene; trust the listener to feel it (the sevdah engine, Module 4.5).
- **Prosody = form matches content.** The *sound and shape* of the words mirror the meaning: short clipped lines for tension, long open lines for release.
- **Stable vs. unstable.** Perfect rhyme + even lines = stable (resolution). Slant rhyme + uneven lines = unstable (tension). Use instability on purpose, resolve it at the hook.
- **Contrast.** Verse vs. chorus must differ — density, rhythm, register, melody. Sameness is death (Playbook §D2).
- **The turn.** The bridge earns its keep by *turning* — a new angle, a confession, a twist — then hands back to the hook.
- **Point of view discipline.** Decide who's talking, to whom, in what tense — and hold it (Playbook §E2).
- **Write to the ear, edit to the page, then back to the ear.** Draft out loud; tighten on paper; confirm out loud against the tempo.
