# Serbian/Bosnian Lyric Writing Practice Curriculum

A 15-exercise progressive curriculum adapted for Serbian/Bosnian lyric writing, based on research into professional songwriting workflows, Pat Pattison's Berklee method, and Balkan corpus analysis.

## Beginner Exercises (4)

### 1. Object Writing
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Time:** 10 minutes daily (morning)
- **Description:** Pick an object at random and write about it using all seven senses — sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch, organic (body internal), and kinesthetic (motion/space). No rhyme, no rhythm, no complete sentences needed. Write freely. Stop at the timer, even mid-sentence. The frustration of stopping trains you to dive deeper faster each day.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Use culturally resonant objects: *rakija* (smell the plum, taste the burn, feel the glass weight, hear the *živeli*), *čaršija* (hear the gossip, smell the coffee, feel the cobblestones), *splav* (feel the river sway, hear the bass through the floor, taste the cheap beer). The goal is to build a sensory vocabulary rooted in Balkan daily life, not generic English-language imagery.

### 2. Alphabet Rhyming
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Time:** 5 minutes daily (morning)
- **Description:** Write the alphabet at the top of a page. Choose a target word and find as many rhymes as possible, listing them under the matching letter. Include near-rhymes and slant rhymes at the bottom of each listing. This builds the rhyme bank that surfaces automatically during freestyling and verse writing.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Organize by Serbian vowels instead of the alphabet. Start with /a/-words: *da, sam, na, kad, sve, ja, brata, vrata, kata, zvala, pala, hala, mala, stala, dala, znala*. Then /o/-words: *noć, moć, tvoj, broj, svoj, gol, pol, sol, bol, stol*. Then /e/-words: *reč, več, leč, peč, teško, veško*. Then /i/-words: *bili, sili,tili, mili, snili, shvili*. Then /u/-words: *ruku, muku, tugu, snegu, begu*. The five-vowel system means five clean columns instead of 26 letters — faster to scan under pressure.

### 3. Speed Drafting
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Time:** 10 minutes
- **Description:** Set a 10-minute timer and write continuously without stopping or editing. No corrections, no looking back. Whatever comes out stays on the page. The goal is to bypass the internal critic and access raw generative output. Quality is irrelevant — momentum is everything.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Draft in Serbian without switching to English for "easier" rhymes. If you get stuck, write the next line in dialect or slang rather than reaching for English. The constraint forces you to mine Serbian's vocabulary rather than defaulting to code-switching.

### 4. Sense Writing
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Time:** 10 minutes
- **Description:** Focus on one sense at a time. Describe an object or place through smell alone, then taste alone, then touch alone. This isolates and strengthens each sensory channel so they combine more richly in full writing.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Rotate through Balkan locations: the smell of *kafana* at midnight (cigarettes, rakija, cologne, sweat), the taste of *ćevapi* at 3 AM (grease, onion, kajmak, bread), the sound of *čaršija* at dawn (brooms, church bells, espresso machines, distant traffic). Each location activates different sense memories rooted in lived Balkan experience.

## Intermediate Exercises (5)

### 5. Word Ladder
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 20–45 minutes
- **Description:** Pick a topic. List verbs in one column and nouns in another. Write lines that connect a verb to a noun it wouldn't normally pair with. The constraint shifts the brain from generative mode to associative, pattern-seeking mode — a different cognitive pathway that often produces surprising imagery.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Use Serbian verbs and nouns from different semantic fields. Verbs: *gazim, padam, letim, gorim, pijem, spavam, bježim, čekam*. Nouns: *asfalt, čaršija, splav, rakija, ekran, zlato, noć, obećanje*. Connect: "Gazim obećanje" or "Pijem ekran." The collisions produce Balkan-specific metaphors.

### 6. Lyric Reimagining
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 30–60 minutes
- **Description:** Take a favorite verse, extract its emotional architecture (the underlying feeling structure, not the words), and rewrite it in completely different imagery and language. Attempt 4+ rewrites. Each attempt should preserve the emotional arc while changing every concrete image.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Reverse-engineer verses by Jala Brat, Buba Corelli, or Senidah. Extract the emotional structure: e.g., Senidah's "Bez tebe" = absence → longing → resignation. Rewrite the same arc using completely different Balkan imagery: instead of a dark room, use a crowded *splav*; instead of silence, use noise. Four attempts, each with different imagery but the same emotional skeleton.

### 7. Rhyme Scheme Switching
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 15 minutes
- **Description:** Take an existing verse and deliberately change its rhyme scheme. Convert AABB to ABAB, or introduce internal rhymes and slant rhymes where there were none. This trains flexibility with rhyme architecture and prevents getting stuck in one pattern.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Take a verse from the corpus (e.g., a Buba Corelli verse with AABB scheme) and convert it to ABAB with internal rhymes on bars 2 and 4. Then convert the same verse to an odd-numbered scheme (AA_A) with an internal rhyme on bar 3. The exercise reveals how rhyme scheme changes the verse's rhythm and feel without changing the content.

### 8. Song Form Blueprinting
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 30 minutes
- **Description:** Analyze a great song's structure — section types, line counts, rhyme schemes, syllable patterns — and use it as a template for your own song with completely different content. This teaches structural intelligence by reverse-engineering proven architectures.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Blueprint a Senidah or Nikolija hit: map the section sequence (Intro → Verse 1 → Pre-chorus → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus → Outro), line counts per section, syllable ranges, and rhyme scheme per section. Then write your own song into that exact blueprint with original content. The structure is proven; your job is to fill it with fresh material.

### 9. Constraint-Based Writing
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 30–60 minutes
- **Description:** Apply a structural constraint and write a complete song within it. Constraints force creative problem-solving by blocking default patterns. Examples: no first person, every line includes a time of day, the song cannot contain a specific word.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Write a complete Serbian verse with the constraint "no English words" — forcing full use of Serbian/Bosnian vocabulary instead of code-switching. Or: "every line must contain a Belgrade/Sarajevo location." Or: "no abstract emotion words (ljubav, bol, tuga) — only concrete objects and actions." The "no English" constraint is particularly powerful for Balkan writers who default to code-switching.

## Advanced Exercises (6)

### 10. Landing the Chorus
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 30 minutes
- **Description:** Practice writing choruses that land emotionally. Use chord techniques, melodic placement, and lyrical positioning to spotlight the key line. The chorus is the song's thesis — every element should converge on the payoff line, ideally placed last.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Write 5 choruses for the same verse, each using a different hook formula from the pop style guide (Formula A: Action + Object, Formula B: Identity Chant, Formula C: Bilingual Phonetic Taunt, Formula D: Negation Loop, Formula E: Rumor/Atmosphere Loop). Test each by singing it over a looped beat. Which one lands hardest? Why? The comparison builds hook intuition.

### 11. Melodic Timing
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 20 minutes
- **Description:** Experiment with placing important words on strong beats. Vary phrasing — front-heavy vs. back-heavy — to create dramatic contrast. Sing the same lyric with different rhythmic placements and notice how meaning shifts with timing.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Take a Serbian verse and place the key noun on the downbeat (front-heavy) in one pass, then after the downbeat (back-heavy) in another. Notice how "Savij moje telo" feels different when "Savij" hits beat 1 vs. when it lands on the "and" of beat 1. Apply Pattison's prosody rules: stressed syllables on stressed beats, /a/ and /o/ vowels on the highest notes.

### 12. Perspective Shifts
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 45 minutes
- **Description:** Write the same song from three different perspectives: first person ("I"), second person ("You"), and third person ("They"). Each perspective opens different story banks and changes what the song can say. Third-person storytelling pulls the ego out and silences the inner critic.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Write a song about a night out at a *splav* in three perspectives: 1st person ("Ja sam ušao u klub i video je"), 2nd person ("Ti si stajala kod šanka"), 3rd person ("Ona je došla sama i otišla sama"). Each version reveals different details — 1st person is subjective, 2nd person is intimate, 3rd person is cinematic. This directly implements Pattison's I-you-we verse development formula.

### 13. Reverse-Engineering
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 30–60 minutes
- **Description:** Analyze professional lyrics for their use of alliteration, repetition, metaphor, internal rhyme, and autobiographical storytelling. Identify each device, then implement those same devices in original work with different content.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Take 8 bars from Jala Brat or Buba Corelli. Mark every rhyme (end and internal), every assonance cluster, every brand reference, every vowel that repeats. Count syllables per line. Identify the rhyme scheme. Then write your own 8 bars using the exact same device inventory — same rhyme scheme density, same syllable count, same number of internal rhymes — but with your own content. This is the fastest way to internalize professional craft.

### 14. Multi-Genre Writing
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 60 minutes
- **Description:** Write the same concept as a pop hook, a rap verse, and a folk-chorus. This reveals how genre conventions shape lyrical choices — syllable density, rhyme complexity, vocabulary register, and emotional directness all change with genre.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Take one concept (e.g., "loyalty on the streets") and write it three ways: (1) as a Senidah-style pop hook (4 lines, 5–6 syllables, ABAB, emotional), (2) as a Jala Brat-style drill verse (8 bars, 8–10 syllables, AABB with internal rhymes, boast-heavy), (3) as a turbo-folk chorus (4 lines, 6–8 syllables, AABB, nostalgic). The same idea in three genres teaches you what is universal vs. what is genre-specific.

### 15. Co-Writing Simulation
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Time:** 60 minutes
- **Description:** Write a verse, then have a collaborator write the next verse without seeing yours. Reconcile the two — adjust pronouns, tense, tone, and narrative to make them cohere. This trains adaptability and the editorial veto, the two most important co-writing skills.
- **Balkan adaptation:** Exchange verses with a co-writer via voice memo. Write verse 1, send a voice memo. Your partner writes verse 2 from the memo alone. Then meet (in person or video call) to reconcile: do the pronouns match? Is the dialect consistent (Ekavian vs. Ijekavian)? Does verse 2 advance what verse 1 set up, or does it rephrase? The reconciliation process is where the real learning happens.

## Daily Practice Routine

### Morning (15 minutes)
- **10 min:** Object writing — pick a random Balkan object, write using all 7 senses, stop at the timer
- **5 min:** Alphabet rhyming — choose one Serbian word, find as many rhymes as possible organized by vowel (/a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/)

### Afternoon or Evening (20–45 minutes)
- **5 min:** Vowel warm-ups — sing on "ah," "oh," and "ee" for 30 seconds each; name 5 objects doing actions
- **15–40 min:** Focused writing exercise from the curriculum (rotate daily):
  - Monday: Speed drafting
  - Tuesday: Word ladder
  - Wednesday: Constraint-based writing
  - Thursday: Lyric reimagining
  - Friday: Landing the chorus
  - Saturday: Reverse-engineering
  - Sunday: Rest or free writing
- **End with:** Record a voice memo of anything usable, no matter how rough

### Weekly
- **One co-writing session** (in person or remote) — exchange verses, reconcile, practice the editorial veto
- **One reverse-engineering session** — analyze a favorite Balkan lyric for craft devices, implement those devices in original work
- **One revision session** — take a previous draft through the Creator/Editor hat method: Creator writes freely (90% power), Editor revises ruthlessly (90% power). Read aloud without melody — if it doesn't hold up as spoken language, the melody is covering structural problems.

### Monthly
- **Genre stretch** — write a song in a genre outside your comfort zone. If you write drill, write a pop ballad. If you write pop, write a trap verse. The stretch reveals assumptions you didn't know you had.
- **Input week** — stop writing for one week. Consume music and literature outside your lane: listen to genres you never play, read poetry you never read, watch films you wouldn't normally choose. Return with fresh material loaded. Brains need new input to produce new output.

## Progression Guidelines

- **Beginner phase (months 1–3):** Focus on exercises 1–4. Build the daily habit of object writing and alphabet rhyming. Don't judge output — judge consistency.
- **Intermediate phase (months 3–12):** Add exercises 5–9. Begin reverse-engineering professional lyrics. Start co-writing sessions.
- **Advanced phase (year 1+):** Integrate exercises 10–15. All exercises remain in rotation. The daily morning routine (object writing + alphabet rhyming) never stops — it is the warm-up that feeds everything else.
- **The 10,000-hour framework** suggests roughly 10 years of consistent, deliberate practice. But quality of practice matters more than quantity: playing through a song without focusing on weak areas isn't deep practice — it's practicing your mistakes.
